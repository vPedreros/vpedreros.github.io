## Overview

Investigating the detectability and imprints of the cosmological frame-dragging (vector) potential within modified theories of gravity. This work studies the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) across large-scale structure surveys to quantify how gravitomagnetic effects can serve as discriminatory signatures of modified gravity against standard General Relativity.

## Motivation

While cosmological perturbation theory often focuses on scalar perturbations, vector perturbations—and particularly the frame-dragging potential—carry distinct signatures of cosmic velocity fields and rotational modes. In modified gravity scenarios, departures from standard Einstein-Hilbert dynamics can modify the growth and coupling of these vector potentials. Quantifying the observational SNR provides concrete criteria for future surveys.

## Methodology

- Numerical computation of perturbation equations and cosmological observables.
- Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) quantification for current and next-generation large-scale structure surveys.
- Comparison between standard $\Lambda\text{CDM}$ (General Relativity) and representative modified gravity models.
